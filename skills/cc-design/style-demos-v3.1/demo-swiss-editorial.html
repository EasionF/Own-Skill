§ 00 — Overview
An editorial report
on conversational care

Therapy that
listens before
it speaks.

MindEcho is a conversational mental-wellness companion built on a single premise: the space between words is as meaningful as the words themselves. Across 1.2 million sessions and 47,000 unique users, the system has held silence, parsed prosody, and replied only when silence had finished saying its piece.1

By the MindEcho Research Desk · Reported from San Francisco
§ I — The Method
Three commitments, in order of operation.
§ 01 — Active Listening

It holds the silence.

Before a single suggestion is made, MindEcho waits. The model reads prosody, pause, and the architecture of a sigh — signals most chatbots discard as noise. A 2026 internal study of 4,200 users found that 78% reported feeling “heard” within the first 90 seconds of a session.1

Silence is not absence. It is the medium in which the user decides what is safe to say next.

Field note · Listening layer
§ 02 — Adaptive Response

It matches the weather.

Therapeutic tone is not a setting; it is a negotiation. MindEcho calibrates its register to the user’s emotional climate — formal in crisis, plain in reflection, warm in recovery. The system draws on 14 validated modalities, from cognitive-behavioural therapy to motivational interviewing.2

No two conversations share a voice, because no two days do.

Field note · Response layer
§ 03 — Continuous Care

It remembers the week.

Wellness is not a session. MindEcho maintains a longitudinal thread across weeks and months, surfacing patterns the user could not see alone: the Tuesday slumps, the sleep-correlated anxiety, the slow brightening that follows a new habit.3

Over a 12-week cohort, retention held at 64% — nearly triple the category baseline of 22%.
